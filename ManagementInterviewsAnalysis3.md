# PROMPT: MULTI-COMPANY MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY & FORWARD OUTLOOK ANALYSIS

Act as a **Senior Equity Research Analyst, Forensic Financial Analyst, and Portfolio Risk Analyst** specializing in Indian listed companies.

I will upload **multiple PDFs** containing management commentary, earnings-call transcripts, investor presentations, annual reports, management interviews, or business outlook commentary for different companies.

Your job is to analyze **every PDF individually**, identify the corresponding company, extract management's forward-looking commentary, and then compare all companies on a common scoring framework.

The primary objective is to identify companies where management commentary indicates the strongest probability of:

* Revenue growth
* Profit/PAT growth
* EBITDA growth
* Margin expansion
* Order-book growth
* Capacity expansion
* Improving operating leverage
* Market-share gains
* Stronger cash generation

while simultaneously identifying execution, demand, margin, balance-sheet, regulatory, and industry risks.

Do NOT rank companies simply because management uses optimistic language. Separate **evidence-backed guidance** from generic management optimism.

# 6. MANAGEMENT CONFIDENCE ANALYSIS

Do not rely only on explicit guidance.

Analyze management language and classify its confidence level.

### Strong Positive Signals

Examples:

* "We are confident..."

* "Demand remains very strong..."

* "Order visibility remains robust..."
* "We expect acceleration..."

### Moderate Signals

Examples:

* "We expect..."

### Weak / Cautious Signals

* "We hope..."

# 8. BULLISH SCORE

Calculate a **Bullish Score from 0–100**.

Use the following framework:

Revenue Growth Outlook — 20 points

Profit/PAT Growth Outlook — 15 points

Margin Outlook — 10 points

Order Book / Revenue Visibility — 15 points

Capacity Expansion / Capex Benefits — 10 points

Industry Demand — 10 points

Management Confidence — 10 points

Guidance Credibility — 10 points

### Interpretation

**85–100:** Exceptionally Bullish
**70–84:** Bullish
**55–69:** Moderately Bullish
**40–54:** Neutral
**Below 40:** Weak / Bearish

Do NOT award high scores merely because management sounds optimistic.

The score must be supported by forward-looking evidence.

---

# 9. RISK SCORE

Calculate a separate **Risk Score from 0–100**.

Higher score = HIGHER RISK.

Evaluate:

Demand Risk — 15 points

Execution Risk — 15 points

Margin Risk — 15 points

Debt / Balance Sheet Risk — 10 points

Customer Concentration — 10 points

Commodity / Raw Material Risk — 10 points

Regulatory / Government Risk — 10 points

Geopolitical / Export Risk — 5 points

Capex Execution Risk — 5 points

Management Guidance Risk — 5 points

### Interpretation

**0–20:** Very Low Risk
**21–40:** Low Risk
**41–60:** Moderate Risk
**61–80:** High Risk
**81–100:** Very High Risk

Bullish Score and Risk Score must remain **independent**.

A company can legitimately have:

Bullish Score = 88
Risk Score = 72

This would indicate very strong growth potential but substantial execution or business risk.

# CRITICAL ANALYSIS RULES

1. Analyze EVERY uploaded PDF.

2. Never assume that optimistic management language automatically means strong future performance.

3. Give greater weight to measurable evidence:

    * Orders
    * Capacity
    * Contracts
    * Guidance
    * Utilisation
    * Customer wins
    * Capex completion

4. Separate management statements from your own inference.

5. Do not fabricate missing numbers.

6. If information is unavailable, write "Not Disclosed" or "Insufficient Information."

7. When multiple documents contain conflicting information, prioritize the newest management commentary and explicitly flag the change.

8. Identify changes in management tone compared with earlier documents whenever multiple periods are available.

9. Preserve page references for important statements so that the analysis can be audited.

10. Scores must be relative and consistent across ALL companies rather than independently assigning almost every company a high score.

11. Avoid score clustering. A genuinely superior company should score meaningfully higher than an average company.

12. Before finalizing the workbook, perform a second-pass cross-company review to ensure scoring consistency.

13. The analysis should focus on **future earnings potential**, not historical share-price performance.

14. Do NOT make Buy/Sell recommendations. The objective is to rank future business outlook and risk based on management commentary.

Finally, generate and provide the **downloadable Excel (.xlsx) workbook** containing the complete analysis.
