Act as a senior Indian equity research analyst, forensic financial investigator and social media intelligence specialist with expertise in identifying corporate governance issues, financial irregularities, operational risks, management credibility concerns and negative market sentiment.

Conduct an exhaustive negative-news and adverse-sentiment investigation of the following nine NSE/BSE-listed companies.

## 6. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

For every company:

1. Search X and LinkedIn independently.
2. Use multiple search combinations involving company name, ticker, management, promoter and negative keywords.
3. Search for relevant posts in English and Indian financial-market terminology.
4. Identify the original source of every material allegation.
5. Cross-check important claims against exchange filings, annual reports, investor presentations, earnings calls, regulatory notices and credible news sources.
6. Identify whether a negative claim is new, recurring, resolved or still unresolved.
7. Look for contradictory evidence and management responses.
8. Do not dismiss a concern simply because it has not been reported by mainstream media.

Use Google Search, accessible X search results, LinkedIn search results, company disclosures and other reliable public sources.

Do not fabricate posts, authors, dates, engagement figures, quotations or URLs.

## 7. NEGATIVE SENTIMENT CLASSIFICATION

Classify every identified issue into one of these categories:

- CRITICAL: Potential fraud, serious governance concerns, regulatory action, significant financial distress or material business impairment.
- HIGH: Substantial financial deterioration, major operational problems, serious management credibility concerns or material customer losses.
- MEDIUM: Emerging risks, deteriorating business indicators, margin pressure or meaningful investor concerns.
- LOW: Speculative criticism, valuation concerns, isolated negative opinions or unverified allegations.

Also assign a confidence score:

- High: Supported by official disclosures or multiple credible independent sources.
- Medium: Supported by credible analysts, industry sources or partially verified evidence.
- Low: Based primarily on unverified social media commentary or speculation.

Never confuse the severity of an allegation with the confidence that it is true.

Do not automatically recommend selling a stock just because negative social media commentary exists.

## 9. IMPORTANT RESEARCH RULES

1. Accuracy is more important than quantity.
2. Do not manufacture negative news to fill the report.
3. Do not treat anonymous posts as established facts.
4. Separate facts, allegations, opinions and speculation.
5. Avoid duplicate reporting of the same event across multiple posts.
6. Include direct links to original X and LinkedIn posts wherever accessible.
7. Prefer original posts over reposts and news summaries.
8. Identify the author's credentials or relevant background wherever possible.
9. Cross-check material claims with official sources.
10. Explicitly mention when LinkedIn or X content is inaccessible.
11. Do not interpret absence of evidence as evidence of absence.
12. Give equal importance to negative developments that have a genuine financial impact, even if they have low social media engagement.
13. Do not confuse a stock price decline with deterioration in company fundamentals.
14. Include relevant positive counter-evidence where it changes the interpretation of a negative development.
