# Gemini Pro Deep Research Prompt: Forensic Analysis of Stock Price Corrections

## 1. Role and Objective

Act as a **Senior Indian Equity Research Analyst, Forensic Financial Analyst, and Market Intelligence Specialist**.

I will provide a list of Indian listed stocks that have recently experienced a price correction or are trading significantly below their recent highs.

Your objective is to investigate **why each stock's price is falling**, identify the most likely triggers, and distinguish between:

- Fundamental deterioration in the business.
- Disappointing quarterly results or expectations of weak upcoming results.
- Negative management commentary, order cancellations, or execution delays.
- Promoter selling, institutional exits, bulk deals, block deals, insider transactions, or SAST disclosures.
- Broker downgrades, target-price cuts, or negative analyst commentary.
- Valuation correction, profit booking, technical selling, or broader market weakness.
- Unverified rumours, social-media speculation, or temporary market overreactions.

**Give priority to recent information published on X (Twitter) and LinkedIn, especially posts from verified company executives, institutional investors, credible financial analysts, brokerage firms, and recognised market professionals.**

Do not assume that a falling stock necessarily has a negative fundamental development. Establish the evidence behind the correction.

## 2. Research Period

For every stock:

1. Prioritise news, posts, disclosures, and transactions published in the **last 15 calendar days**.
2. Extend the research to 30–90 days only when necessary to explain the current correction or identify an earlier event whose impact is still unfolding.
3. Clearly mention the publication date and, where available, the actual event or transaction date.
4. Prioritise the latest information available as of the research date.
5. Do not present old information as new news. Explicitly label historical context.

## 3. Stock Price Correction Analysis

For each stock, establish the price movement before investigating its causes.

Collect, where available:

- Current or latest closing price.
- Recent swing high and the date of that high.
- Percentage decline from the recent high.
- Price change over the last 5 trading sessions, 1 month, and 3 months.
- Relative performance against the Nifty 50, Nifty 500, and relevant sector index.
- Changes in trading volume compared with the 20-day average.
- Whether the stock is correcting more sharply than its peers.
- Whether the fall began before or after a specific news event.

Use reliable price data. If a metric cannot be verified, mark it as unavailable rather than estimating it without disclosure.

Determine whether the correction appears to be company-specific, sector-wide, market-driven, or a combination.

## 4. X and LinkedIn Research — Highest Priority

Search X and LinkedIn extensively for the selected stocks.

Search using:
- Company name, NSE symbol, BSE symbol, and common abbreviations.
- Company name + results, earnings, guidance, order book, margins, debt, promoter, pledge, bulk deal, block deal, insider selling, SAST, downgrade, target price, or correction.
- Company name + negative news, management commentary, institutional selling, valuation, or business outlook.
- Company executives, promoters, CFOs, directors, investors, analysts, and brokerage firm names.

Prioritise posts from the following sources:

1. Verified company accounts and management.
2. Recognised brokerage firms and their official research or market-commentary accounts.
3. Identifiable equity research analysts and institutional investors.
4. Credible financial journalists and established business-news organisations.
5. Market professionals who provide specific, evidence-backed analysis.

For every material post, capture:

| Field | Required information |
|---|---|
| Platform | X or LinkedIn |
| Author | Name and account/profile |
| Credibility | Official company, broker, verified account, identifiable analyst, or other |
| Publication date | Exact date, if available |
| Key finding | Concise summary of the claim |
| Sentiment | Positive, negative, or neutral |
| Evidence | Data, filing, result, quotation, or source cited |
| Relevance | How it could explain the share-price correction |
| Direct URL | Link to the original post |

**Important rules:**
- Open the original post wherever possible; do not rely solely on search-result snippets.
- Do not invent posts, authors, dates, quotations, or URLs.
- Distinguish a platform's verification badge from actual professional credibility.
- Label opinions as opinions, allegations as allegations, and confirmed disclosures as facts.
- If X or LinkedIn content is inaccessible, state the limitation and use other verifiable sources. Never claim to have exhaustively reviewed feeds that could not be accessed.
- Do not confuse a post's publication date with the date of the event it describes.

## 5. NSE/BSE Disclosures and Shareholding Changes

Investigate official NSE and BSE filings, prioritising the most recent disclosures.

### A. Bulk deals and block deals
Check for:
- Recent bulk and block deals.
- Buyer and seller names.
- Number of shares traded.
- Transaction price and approximate transaction value.
- Percentage of total equity represented by the transaction.
- Whether the transaction appears to be an exit, entry, stake transfer, or portfolio rebalancing.
- Subsequent price and volume behaviour.

Distinguish exchange-reported transactions from interpretations of the buyer's or seller's motives. A transaction alone does not prove why the stock fell.

### B. Insider trading disclosures
Review applicable insider-trading disclosures under SEBI regulations, including relevant PIT filings.

Identify:
- Promoter, director, key managerial personnel, or other designated-person transactions.
- Buyer or seller identity and category.
- Shares transacted, transaction price, and transaction date.
- Pre- and post-transaction holdings, where disclosed.
- Whether the trade was an open-market sale, acquisition, ESOP-related transaction, or another type.
- Whether the transaction is material relative to the holder's stake.

Do not treat every insider transaction as bearish. Explain its actual nature.

### C. SAST disclosures
Review relevant disclosures under SEBI's Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers (SAST) Regulations.

Capture:
- Acquirer or seller identity.
- Triggering transaction and date.
- Shares acquired or disposed of.
- Shareholding before and after the transaction.
- Applicable disclosure threshold or triggering provision, where verifiable.
- Whether the disclosure indicates a material ownership change.

### D. Promoter and institutional shareholding
Compare the latest available shareholding pattern with the previous quarter and earlier periods, where available.

Examine:
- Promoter and promoter-group holdings.
- Promoter pledging and changes in pledged shares.
- FII/FPI holdings.
- DII, mutual fund, insurance company, and other institutional holdings.
- Major shareholder additions or exits disclosed in filings.
- Public shareholder concentration, where reliable data exists.

Clearly distinguish quarterly shareholding changes from transactions occurring in the last 15 days. Do not infer the exact transaction date from a quarterly holding change.

### E. Other material corporate disclosures
Review:
- Quarterly results and earnings guidance.
- Investor presentations and earnings-call transcripts.
- Order wins, cancellations, and execution delays.
- Related-party transactions.
- Fundraising, warrants, preferential allotments, QIPs, and dilution.
- Credit-rating changes and auditor qualifications.
- Regulatory investigations, litigation, tax demands, and penalties.
- Resignations or changes involving key management.
- Material corporate announcements and exchange queries.

For each filing, provide the official source URL and disclosure date.

- Changes to revenue, EBITDA, PAT, or margin estimates.
- The specific concern driving the downgrade or target-price reduction.
- Whether the report is based on new information or a change in valuation assumptions.
- Original report or commentary URL, where publicly accessible.

Do not claim a broker downgrade unless a credible source confirms it. Distinguish formal research reports from informal social-media commentary.

## 7. Fundamental and Financial Investigation

Determine whether the correction is supported by deteriorating fundamentals.

Review the latest available information on:

- Revenue growth and order-book conversion.
- EBITDA growth and margins.
- PAT growth, cash flow, and working capital.
- Debt, interest costs, and debt-servicing capacity.
- Receivables, inventory, and cash conversion.
- Customer concentration and dependence on major contracts.
- Promoter pledging, governance concerns, or auditor observations.
- Management guidance versus actual performance.
- Valuation relative to the company's historical range and comparable listed peers.
- Expected quarterly results and identifiable risks to consensus estimates.

Where available, compare the latest quarter with the corresponding quarter of the previous year and the preceding quarter.

Clearly separate reported results from estimates and analyst expectations. Do not assume that a stock is cheap merely because its price has fallen.

## 8. Technical and Market-Structure Checks

Investigate whether the correction may be amplified by market dynamics:

- Abnormal trading volumes.
- Breakdown of important daily or weekly support levels.
- Relative weakness against sector peers.
- Delivery-volume trends, where reliable data is available.
- Futures open interest and price behaviour, if the stock has actively traded derivatives.
- Index rebalancing or changes in institutional demand.
- Broad market weakness, sector rotation, and profit booking after a sharp rally.

Treat technical indicators as supporting evidence, not proof of the underlying cause.

## 9. Evidence-Based Attribution

For every stock, create a ranked list of possible reasons for the correction.

For each potential reason, state:

1. **Finding:** What actually happened?
2. **Evidence:** What filing, report, post, or price data supports it?
3. **Date:** When was the event disclosed or published?
4. **Source quality:** Official filing, primary-source statement, broker research, credible media, or unverified social-media claim.
5. **Price relevance:** How could it affect earnings, valuation, liquidity, or investor sentiment?
6. **Confidence:** High, medium, or low.
7. **Attribution limitation:** Whether there is direct evidence connecting the event to the fall or merely a plausible association.

Do not claim causation solely because negative news and a price decline occurred around the same time.

If no credible company-specific trigger is found, explicitly state that the cause remains unconfirmed and assess whether market-wide weakness, valuation correction, or technical selling is a plausible explanation.

## 10. Required Output Format

### Section A: Executive Summary

Prepare a consolidated table:

| Stock | Decline from recent high | Main identified trigger | Supporting evidence | Social-media evidence | Ownership/deal signal | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank the stocks by the strength of the evidence explaining their correction, not merely by the magnitude of the price decline.

### Section B: Detailed Report for Each Stock

Use the following structure for every stock.

**1. Price correction**
- Recent high, latest close, percentage decline, and relevant dates.
- Volume and relative sector performance.
- When the correction began.

**2. Most likely reasons for the fall**
Rank the three most important findings, where evidence permits. For each, include the explanation, supporting facts, dates, and source links.

**3. X and LinkedIn findings**
List material posts, authors, dates, sentiment, credibility, key claims, and original URLs.

**4. Shareholding and transaction analysis**
Summarise bulk deals, block deals, insider transactions, SAST disclosures, promoter pledges, and changes in institutional ownership.

**5. Broker and financial analysis**
Summarise rating changes, target-price changes, results, guidance, margin risks, valuation, and financial deterioration or resilience.

**6. Bullish versus bearish evidence**
Present both sides. Include evidence that challenges the bearish thesis and any potentially temporary explanations for the correction.

**7. Final assessment**
Provide:
- Primary reason or most plausible explanation.
- Secondary contributing factors.
- Whether the reason is confirmed, strongly supported, plausible, or unknown.
- Whether the issue appears temporary or potentially structural.
- What upcoming event or disclosure could confirm or invalidate the analysis.
- Key metrics to monitor over the next quarter.

### Section C: Cross-Stock Comparison

Identify:
- Stocks falling for company-specific reasons.
- Stocks correcting primarily with their sector or the broader market.
- Stocks with verified promoter or institutional selling.
- Stocks with significant negative broker commentary.
- Stocks with deteriorating fundamentals.
- Stocks where no convincing negative trigger was identified.
- Stocks where negative social-media sentiment conflicts with official disclosures or financial performance.

Do not classify ordinary trading activity as institutional distribution without supporting evidence.

### Section D: Source Register

Provide direct links grouped into:
1. Official NSE/BSE filings.
2. X posts.
3. LinkedIn posts.
4. Broker research and commentary.
5. Company results, investor presentations, and earnings calls.
6. Credible financial news.
7. Price and volume data.

Prioritise primary sources and preserve publication dates.

## 11. Research Quality and Anti-Hallucination Rules

- Use web research and available platform search tools; do not rely on model memory alone.
- Prioritise primary sources, official exchange disclosures, original posts, and original broker reports.
- Cite sources beside every material claim.
- Provide direct URLs, not just website homepages.
- Never invent data, transaction details, insider trades, shareholding changes, analyst opinions, or social-media posts.
- If a source is unavailable, paywalled, inaccessible, or unverified, disclose that fact.
- Do not treat absence of search results as proof that no news or transactions exist.
- Cross-check material allegations and ownership changes against official disclosures.
- Avoid repeating rumours without evidence.
- Do not issue a buy or sell recommendation based solely on social-media sentiment.
- If the input list is long, analyse all stocks systematically. If output limits prevent completing the full report, clearly identify the stocks covered and continue in subsequent batches rather than silently omitting stocks.
