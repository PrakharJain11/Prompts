## 3. Define Horizontal Support

A horizontal support zone is a price area where a stock has repeatedly attracted buying interest or where downward price movements have previously reversed.

Identify support using the following evidence:

### A. Multiple historical touches
- Look for at least 2–3 meaningful weekly swing lows around a similar price level.
- Prefer support zones tested on separate occasions rather than several consecutive candles forming one low.
- A touch is valid when price approaches or enters the identified support zone and subsequently moves meaningfully upward.

### B. Support-zone calculation
For each stock:
1. Identify significant weekly swing lows over the historical lookback.
2. Cluster swing lows occurring near similar price levels into potential horizontal support zones.
3. Estimate the support zone's lower boundary, upper boundary, and central reference price.
4. Explain how the zone was identified and how many independent tests support its validity.

Use a reasonable tolerance when grouping historical lows, typically around 1–3%, adjusted for the stock's volatility and price behaviour. Do not force unrelated swing lows into the same zone.

### C. Strength of support
Evaluate each support zone using:
- Number of independent historical tests.
- Recency of the tests.
- Magnitude of upward reactions following previous tests.
- Weekly closing behaviour near the zone.
- Volume during previous tests and subsequent rebounds.
- Whether the zone has already been decisively broken.
- Whether the same zone acted as resistance before becoming support.

A support zone is stronger when multiple independent tests have produced meaningful upward reactions without a sustained breakdown.

## 4. Determine Whether the Current Price Is Near Support

Calculate:

**Distance from support (%)**

\[
\frac{\text{Current Price}-\text{Support Reference Price}}
{\text{Support Reference Price}}\times100
\]

Use the following initial classification:

- **Very near support:** Current price is within 0–2% above the support reference price.
- **Near support:** Current price is more than 2% and up to 5% above support.
- **Inside support zone:** Current price is within the identified support zone.
- **Support at risk:** Price is testing support but has shown signs of weakening or is approaching a breakdown.
- **Broken support:** A completed weekly candle has closed decisively below the support zone.
- **No valid nearby support:** No sufficiently validated horizontal support exists within 5% below the current price.

Treat these as screening thresholds, not universal trading rules. Adjust them where justified by the stock's volatility and the width of the support zone, and disclose any adjustments.

Do not classify a stock trading materially below support as being near support simply because its price is close to the old level in absolute rupees.

## 6. Volume and Price-Action Confirmation

Assess the following supporting signals without making them mandatory:
- Volume contraction as price approaches support.
- High-volume buying or a strong rejection candle near support.
- Long lower wick on a weekly candle.
- Bullish weekly reversal or bullish engulfing pattern near support.
- A higher low forming near the support zone.
- Increased volume on rebounds compared with declines.

Flag negative evidence:
- Repeated weak rebounds from the same zone.
- Increasing selling volume near support.
- Strong bearish weekly candles closing near their lows.
- A completed weekly close below support.
- A failed retest of previously broken support.

Do not label a stock as bullish merely because its price is near support.
