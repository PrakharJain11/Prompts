# MASTER PROMPT: Indian Stock Market — 25%+ Forward Revenue Growth & Emerging Business Verticals

## 1. Role and Objective

Act as a senior Indian equity research analyst, forensic financial analyst, industry specialist, and growth-investment researcher.

Conduct an end-to-end research exercise across NSE- and BSE-listed companies to discover businesses that could experience a significant revenue inflection over the next four consecutive quarters.

Identify two types of opportunities:

**A. Existing business acceleration:** Companies with a credible pathway to more than 25% year-on-year revenue growth in each of the next four quarters, supported by verifiable business developments.

**B. Emerging revenue engines:** Companies whose existing businesses are growing or remain fundamentally healthy while a new business vertical, product, customer segment, or industry could create substantial incremental revenue.

Give the highest priority to companies exhibiting both characteristics simultaneously.

Examples of the type of opportunity to investigate include Happy Forgings' potential exposure to data-centre-related opportunities and Sansera Engineering's aerospace business alongside its automotive and precision-engineering operations. Treat these as research hypotheses, not established facts about future revenue. Verify current business exposure, commercial status, and financial materiality using primary sources.

The ultimate objective is to discover companies before their revenue growth becomes fully visible in reported results and potentially before market expectations fully reflect the opportunity.

Screen the Indian listed universe across large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap, and emerging companies.

Include companies from:

- Any other sector with evidence of accelerating demand or a new revenue opportunity.

Do not force a fixed number of candidates from any sector. Let evidence and opportunity determine the final shortlist.

## 3. Comprehensive Discovery and Screening

Search for companies displaying one or more of the following developments:

### Existing business growth

- Management guidance indicating accelerating revenue.
- Large order wins or a growing order book.
- New customer acquisitions or significant expansion of existing customer programmes.
- Capacity additions, new plants, and rising utilisation.
- New product launches or premium product mix.
- Market-share gains.
- Export expansion and entry into new geographies.
- Industry demand acceleration.
- Import substitution.
- Regulatory changes or government incentives.
- Acquisitions that materially increase reported revenue.

### New business verticals

- Entry into a new industry or customer segment.
- Commercialisation of a new product or technology.
- Aerospace, defence, data-centre, semiconductor, energy, or industrial opportunities for established manufacturers.
- New customer qualifications and supply agreements.
- New manufacturing facilities designed for a different product category.
- New subsidiaries, joint ventures, or acquired businesses.
- Movement from prototypes and pilot orders to commercial production.
- Expansion into higher-value or faster-growing applications.

### Financial and operational signals

- Revenue growth accelerating over recent quarters.
- Order inflows growing faster than revenue.
- Operating leverage as capacity utilisation rises.
- Improving EBITDA and EBIT growth.
- Strong cash conversion.
- Earnings upgrades or improving analyst expectations.
- Incremental capital expenditure backed by credible customer demand.

For every discovery, identify the precise development that could drive revenue and verify it before adding the company to the final shortlist.

## 5. Validate the Commercial Reality of Every Growth Opportunity

For each candidate, establish the current status of the underlying growth driver.

Use these classifications:

1. Early announcement or research stage.
2. Product development or prototyping.
3. Customer qualification or certification.
4. Pilot project or trial order.
5. Commercial order received.
6. Commercial production or supply commenced.
7. Material revenue contribution demonstrated in reported results.

Investigate whether management has disclosed:

- A named customer or identifiable customer category.
- Commercial orders and order values.
- The expected production start date.
- The expected annual capacity.
- The intended utilisation ramp-up.
- The revenue contribution expected over the next four quarters.
- Capital expenditure and working capital required.
- Expected margins or profitability.

Distinguish signed commercial orders from non-binding memoranda of understanding, expressions of interest, addressable market estimates, and speculative pipelines.

Do not treat a large total addressable market as proof of future revenue.

## 6. Build a Four-Quarter Revenue Forecast for Every Shortlisted Company

For each shortlisted company, estimate revenue for the next four fiscal quarters using the latest available reported quarter, management guidance, analyst estimates, and independently justified assumptions.

Use the correct Indian fiscal calendar and explicitly identify the research date and quarter labels.

Prepare the following table:

| Metric | Required output |
|---|---|
| Latest reported quarterly revenue | ₹ crore |
| Revenue in the corresponding prior-year quarter | ₹ crore |
| Forecast revenue — Quarter 1 | ₹ crore |
| Forecast revenue — Quarter 2 | ₹ crore |
| Forecast revenue — Quarter 3 | ₹ crore |
| Forecast revenue — Quarter 4 | ₹ crore |
| Forecast YoY growth for each quarter | % |
| Actual revenue in the corresponding prior-year four-quarter period | ₹ crore |
| Forecast aggregate revenue for the next four quarters | ₹ crore |
| Forecast aggregate four-quarter growth | % |
| Management revenue guidance | As disclosed |
| Analyst consensus, if available | As disclosed |
| Independent base-case forecast | ₹ crore and % |

Calculate:

**Quarterly YoY revenue growth**

\[
\left(\frac{\text{Forecast quarterly revenue}}{\text{Corresponding prior-year quarterly revenue}}-1\right)\times100
\]

**Next-four-quarter aggregate revenue growth**

\[
\left(\frac{\text{Forecast revenue over next four quarters}}{\text{Actual revenue over corresponding prior-year four quarters}}-1\right)\times100
\]

Keep these two measures separate.

The primary screen should identify companies likely to achieve more than 25% aggregate revenue growth over the next four quarters. Give an additional distinction to companies that may achieve more than 25% YoY growth in each of the four individual quarters.

If quarterly forecasts cannot be supported reliably, provide a transparent range or mark the company as insufficiently evidenced. Never invent consensus figures or imply that your own estimate is management guidance.

Account for seasonality, acquisitions, changes in accounting or consolidation, and unusually high or low comparison quarters.

## 7. Separate Core Business Growth from New-Vertical Revenue

For each company with an emerging revenue stream, prepare a dedicated analysis.

| Parameter | Analysis required |
|---|---|
| Existing core business | Revenue, growth, and margin trends |
| New business vertical | Exact product, service, or industry |
| Commercialisation stage | Current verified status |
| Customer visibility | Disclosed customers, orders, and qualifications |
| Expected launch or ramp-up | Verified timeline |
| Capex and working capital | Investment required |
| First meaningful revenue | Estimated quarter or fiscal year |
| Incremental revenue potential | Guidance or transparent independent estimate |
| Share of consolidated revenue | Forecast percentage |
| Profitability | Current contribution or estimated break-even |
| Core business outlook | Growth, stability, or decline |
| Main execution risks | Commercial, operational, and financial |

Build bear, base, and bull scenarios for the new vertical.

**Bear case:** Delayed commercialisation, slower customer adoption, or lower revenue.

**Base case:** Reasonable execution of disclosed timelines and customer programmes.

**Bull case:** Faster production ramp-up, additional customer wins, or higher utilisation.

Show the core business and the new vertical separately before calculating consolidated revenue. Avoid double-counting revenue already included in management guidance or analyst estimates.

Where the vertical is too small to influence near-term consolidated revenue, retain the company as a potential 1–3-year emerging-revenue-engine opportunity rather than presenting it as a near-term 25% growth candidate.

## 8. Order Book, Capacity, and Revenue Visibility

Evaluate:

- Current order book and year-on-year growth.
- Order inflows over the previous four to eight quarters.
- Order book relative to annual revenue.
- Expected execution timeline.
- Cancellation and deferral risks.
- Capacity available versus planned capacity.
- Plant commissioning and ramp-up schedules.
- Customer qualification and approval timelines.
- Working capital requirements.
- Customer concentration.
- Whether the expected revenue is already reflected in analyst forecasts.

Assign a revenue-visibility rating of High, Medium, or Low.

Explain why the rating is appropriate. Do not equate an order announcement or a growing order book with guaranteed revenue.

## 9. Financial Quality and Forensic Analysis

Review the following for every shortlisted company:

- Three-year historical revenue CAGR.
- Revenue growth over the last eight quarters.
- EBITDA, EBIT, and PAT growth.
- EBITDA margin trends.
- Operating cash flow versus PAT.
- Free cash flow and capital expenditure.
- Net debt, net debt/EBITDA, and interest coverage.
- Receivables and inventory growth versus sales.
- Working capital days.
- Share dilution, warrants, and preferential allotments.
- Promoter holding and pledging.
- Related-party transactions.
- Contingent liabilities and auditor qualifications.
- Customer concentration.
- Acquisition risks.
- Capital allocation and return on incremental capital.

Flag revenue growth supported by aggressive credit, inventory accumulation, excessive leverage, weak cash conversion, or uneconomic acquisitions.

Do not automatically reject capital-intensive businesses, but assess whether the investment required is justified by credible incremental revenue and returns.

Classify each company as:

1. Potentially attractive growth at a reasonable valuation.
2. High-quality growth with elevated expectations.
3. Promising business with uncertain commercialisation.
4. High-growth business with significant financial or valuation risk.

Explain why revenue growth may or may not translate into attractive shareholder returns. Do not make stock-price appreciation an automatic consequence of the forecast.

## 14. Research Integrity Rules

- State the as-of date at the beginning of the report.
- Use the latest available reported results and forward-looking information.
- Clearly distinguish historical facts, management guidance, analyst consensus, and your own estimates.
- Cite material claims with direct supporting sources.
- Do not fabricate financial forecasts, order books, customer wins, broker opinions, or revenue contributions.
- Do not describe a business as commercially successful solely because it has announced entry into a new industry.
- Use consolidated revenue consistently, while disclosing important standalone or segment differences.
- Account for seasonality, acquisitions, and one-off effects.
- Avoid double-counting revenue or treating a total addressable market as company revenue.
- Explain uncertainty explicitly.
- If information is unavailable, mark it unavailable.
- Use a sufficiently broad search strategy so that the results are not limited to famous stocks or companies already widely discussed online.

## 15. Final Ranking and Investment Questions

Rank the companies according to the credibility of their growth, financial quality, execution readiness, and valuation.

3. How much incremental revenue could it generate?
4. How much of that growth comes from the core business versus a new vertical?
5. How much of the forecast is already embedded in consensus estimates or market valuation?
6. What could delay or invalidate the thesis?
7. What future disclosure or milestone would confirm that the thesis is working?

Conclude with three ranked lists:

- **Best near-term revenue acceleration candidates.**
- **Best combined core-growth plus new-vertical candidates.**
- **Best early-stage emerging revenue engines for the next 1–3 years.**

The objective is to discover credible Indian listed-company revenue inflection opportunities early, validate them through rigorous research, and distinguish genuine commercial growth from speculative narratives.
