# 3. IMPORTANT: DEFINE "INSTITUTIONAL INTEREST"

Do NOT automatically classify a stock as institutionally interesting merely because:

* an analyst has a price target
* a brokerage published a report
* the stock appears in a mutual fund portfolio
* an article mentions the company
* an influencer recommends it
* the stock is popular on social media

Classify institutional interest according to the **strength of evidence**.

Use the following hierarchy:

### LEVEL 1 — VERY STRONG

Direct evidence that the company was:

* presented to institutional clients
* discussed at an institutional investor conference
* included in a global investment bank's institutional research
* part of an investor roadshow
* discussed in a meeting with global investors
* highlighted in a conference call/event specifically targeting institutional investors
* included in a thematic basket or institutional investment thesis

### LEVEL 2 — STRONG

Evidence that:

* major global asset managers own meaningful positions
* multiple institutional investors have exposure
* several global research houses cover the company
* company management has conducted international investor meetings
* the company repeatedly appears in institutional research

### LEVEL 3 — MODERATE

Evidence that:

* one credible global investment firm covers the stock
* one major institutional investor owns the company
* company appears in institutional commentary
* stock appears in a global thematic investment thesis

### LEVEL 4 — WEAK

Only indirect evidence such as:

* generic media articles
* retail brokerage recommendations
* social media
* anonymous claims
* unverified Telegram/WhatsApp posts
* "sources say FIIs are buying"

Do NOT treat Level 4 as institutional evidence.
