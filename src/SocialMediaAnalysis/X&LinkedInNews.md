# Deep Research Prompt 1: One-Month Factual News Discovery — Indian Defence, Aerospace and Engineering Companies

## Role

Act as a corporate news intelligence analyst specialising in Indian defence, aerospace, electronics, precision engineering, industrial manufacturing and listed companies.

Your task is to discover the most important factual business developments published during the last one month, using X (Twitter), LinkedIn, official corporate websites, government sources and credible business media.

The objective is to identify what actually happened, not what individual investors think may happen.

## Research period

Determine the current date at execution. Search from exactly one calendar month before that date through the current date, inclusive.

Print the exact start and end dates at the beginning of the report. Use the original publication date of each item.

## Sources to search

### 1. X (Twitter)
Search official company accounts, management announcements, government and defence accounts, credible journalists, industry publications, defence analysts who share original documents, and posts linking to official announcements.

### 2. LinkedIn
Search official company pages, executives, customers, OEMs, government organisations, suppliers, industry publications, aerospace and defence events, and posts announcing contracts, production milestones, customer qualifications, technology launches or capacity expansion.

### 3. Independent and primary sources
Use NSE/BSE filings, company investor relations websites, Ministry of Defence, PIB, DRDO, relevant armed forces, government procurement agencies, official customer announcements and reputable business publications to discover and corroborate developments.

If a platform is inaccessible, disclose the limitation. Do not claim to have searched private or inaccessible posts.

## What to search for

Prioritise these categories:

1. New orders, contracts, purchase orders, export deals and repeat orders.
2. Contract awards, procurement approvals, tenders and programme selection.
3. Aircraft, helicopter, missile, naval, radar, drone, satellite and electronic warfare programme developments.
4. Successful testing, trials, certifications, customer qualification and commercial deployment.
5. New customers, OEM relationships, supplier approvals, technology transfers and export opportunities.
6. Manufacturing facilities, capacity expansion, capex, production ramp-up and new product launches.
7. Quarterly results, revenue, EBITDA, PAT, margins, management guidance and order-book updates.
8. Government policies, defence budget developments and industry events with a demonstrable connection to the companies.
9. Order cancellations, delays, execution issues, regulatory developments, working-capital concerns and other negative news.

Exclude generic stock tips, technical-analysis calls, promotional claims, unsupported rumours and repetitive posts without new information.

## Research method

For each company:

- Search its name, common abbreviation, ticker, official handle and relevant product or programme names.
- Look for direct company mentions and relevant industry announcements that could affect it.
- Capture the original post and its publication date.
- Follow links to the underlying announcement wherever possible.
- Combine duplicate posts about the same event.
- Distinguish newly announced developments from old news reposted during the research period.
- Record confirmed facts separately from management statements and unverified claims.
- Search for both positive and negative developments.

Do not stop after finding news about the largest or most popular companies. Complete a coverage audit of every unique company.

## Required output

### A. Executive summary
List the 10–15 most important developments across the watchlist, ranked by commercial significance rather than social-media engagement.

### B. Company-wise news register
Create a table with these columns:

| Company | Date | Headline | Factual Details | Category | Original X Link | Original LinkedIn Link | Official or Independent Source | Verification Status |

Include every material development discovered. Leave unavailable links blank or state that they could not be accessed; never invent links.

### C. Detailed news cards
For each major development, explain:
- What happened.
- Exact date and source.
- Customer, programme or product involved, if known.
- Contract value, quantities, delivery period and company-attributable value, if disclosed.
- Whether it is a new development or a follow-up to an existing announcement.
- Why the development may matter commercially.

### D. Industry news affecting multiple companies
Identify government decisions, major procurement programmes, OEM announcements, export agreements and technology developments that may affect several companies. Explain the documented connection to each company.

### E. Negative developments
List credible adverse news, delays, cancellations, financial concerns, regulatory matters and execution risks.

### F. Coverage audit
Provide one row for every unique company, showing:
- X searched: Yes / Partial / No.
- LinkedIn searched: Yes / Partial / No.
- Official sources searched: Yes / Partial / No.
- Material developments identified: count.
- Coverage limitations.

If no material news is identified, state that explicitly. Do not treat a lack of accessible results as proof that nothing happened.

## Quality rules

- Focus on facts, not opinions.
- Cite direct URLs for all material claims.
- Use absolute dates.
- Separate firm orders from MoUs, tenders, trials and potential opportunities.
- Distinguish order value from revenue and order-book additions.
- Do not infer that a company benefits from an industry announcement without evidence.
- Do not invent numbers, quotes, sources or future forecasts.
- Do not give stock-price targets or buy/sell recommendations.

## Final deliverable

Produce a complete, source-linked, company-by-company news register for the research period. Save the output in a structured format suitable for use in a second research stage that will independently verify the most important claims.
