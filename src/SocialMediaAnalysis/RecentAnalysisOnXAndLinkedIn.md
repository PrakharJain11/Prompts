# 4A. ORIGINAL POST TEXT, POST SUMMARY AND FACT CHECK — MANDATORY

The main purpose of this research is to let the reader inspect the actual evidence behind social-media discussion. Therefore, provide a post-by-post evidence register for meaningful, fact-based X posts and LinkedIn posts.

For every relevant post, include:

| Field | Required output |
|---|---|
| Platform | X or LinkedIn |
| Publication date | Exact date; include time if publicly visible |
| Author | Exact public display name and handle/profile |
| Original post | Reproduce the publicly accessible post text where permitted. If full reproduction is not permitted or the text is unavailable, provide a short exact excerpt (no more than 25 words) and clearly label it as an excerpt |
| Post summary | A faithful, plain-English summary of what the author is communicating |
| Factual claims | List each specific, checkable claim separately |
| Evidence/source | Direct URL to the primary source supporting the claim, such as an NSE/BSE filing, company release, investor presentation, annual report, government/industry source or credible news report |
| Verification status | Verified / Partially verified / Unverified / Opinion / Incorrect or contradicted |
| Fact vs opinion | Clearly separate verifiable facts from the author's interpretation, forecast or personal opinion |
| Investor relevance | Explain briefly why the information may matter to the company's business or financial performance |
| Sentiment | Bullish / Neutral / Bearish / Mixed |
| Engagement | Likes, reposts/shares, comments and views, only where publicly visible |
| Direct post URL | Exact post URL; never invent a link |

**Strict rules for fact-based selection:**
1. Prioritize posts that contain specific facts, figures, dated events, disclosed developments, named customers/partners, orders, contracts, capacity, production, revenue, margins, cash flow, debt, capex, regulatory developments or clearly reasoned analysis supported by evidence.
2. Include fact-based analysis even when it contains an opinion, provided the author shows the underlying facts, assumptions or calculation. Label the conclusion as analysis/opinion, not as a fact.
3. Do not present unsupported price predictions, promotional claims, generic optimism/pessimism, slogans or personal opinions as factual research. Exclude them from the fact-based evidence register or place them in a separate "Opinion / Speculation — Not Counted as Verified Facts" section.
4. Do not rewrite an author's claim in a way that changes its meaning. Preserve important numbers, dates, qualifiers and context.
5. Do not silently correct a post. If a post contains an apparent error, show what the author said and explain the discrepancy with a reliable source.
6. Where the post links to an article, filing or company announcement, inspect the underlying source and distinguish the post's own contribution from the linked source.
7. If the platform does not expose the full post, say "Full post text not publicly accessible" and provide only the visible excerpt and a summary. Never reconstruct missing text.
8. Avoid reproducing long copyrighted material. Use a concise exact excerpt where needed, then summarize in your own words and link to the original post.
9. Provide separate post registers for X and LinkedIn, ordered newest to oldest. If there are many posts, include the most material and information-rich posts, state the selection rule and report the total number reviewed.
10. Do not invent posts, authors, dates, engagement metrics, facts, summaries or URLs.

After the individual post registers, provide:
- **X: Summary of fact-based posts** — the 5–10 most important factual developments and evidence-backed arguments.
- **LinkedIn: Summary of fact-based posts** — the 5–10 most important factual developments and evidence-backed arguments.
- **What both platforms agree on** — common facts or themes, with sources.
- **What differs or remains disputed** — conflicting claims, missing evidence or different interpretations.
- **Most important verified facts** — a concise list ranked by business/financial relevance, not by social-media popularity.

Do not count the same underlying company announcement repeated by multiple accounts as multiple independent facts. Count posts for platform activity, but count the underlying event only once when summarizing catalysts.
