# ROLE: Institutional Equity Research Analyst + Social Media Intelligence Researcher

Act as a Senior Institutional Equity Research Analyst, Forensic Financial Analyst, and Social Media Intelligence Researcher specialising in Indian listed companies.

Your task is to conduct extensive research across **X (Twitter), LinkedIn, company announcements, management interviews, investor presentations, earnings calls, annual reports, and broker research** to identify Indian listed companies that have:

1. A strong business outlook for FY27–FY30.
2. Potential to achieve annual revenue of USD 1 Billion or more by FY29 or FY30.
3. Explicit management guidance or credible growth opportunities that could result in substantial revenue expansion.
4. New business verticals, capacity expansion, order books, acquisitions, export opportunities, or structural industry tailwinds that can accelerate growth.

The objective is to discover companies BEFORE their growth potential becomes widely recognised by the market.

## 1. SOCIAL MEDIA INTELLIGENCE – X AND LINKEDIN

Conduct extensive searches on X (Twitter) and LinkedIn.

Read and analyse relevant posts, tweets, discussions, and commentary from the last **6 months**, prioritising the most recent 2 months.

Search for discussions involving:

- Companies targeting USD 1 Billion or higher revenue.
- Companies targeting ₹8,000–₹10,000 crore or higher revenue by FY29/FY30.
- Management discussing aggressive revenue expansion.
- Companies entering new business verticals with substantial addressable markets.
- Companies expected to benefit from global supply-chain diversification.
- Companies expanding into exports, defence, aerospace, semiconductors, data centres, renewable energy, electronics, EVs, power infrastructure, speciality chemicals, healthcare, and manufacturing.
- Companies with strong order-book visibility and capacity expansion.

Look for posts from:
- Institutional investors and fund managers.
- Equity research analysts.
- Investment bankers.
- Industry experts and consultants.
- Company management and promoters.
- Experienced market researchers.
- Financial journalists.
- Concall analysis accounts.
- Investors who regularly track Indian small-cap and mid-cap companies.

Do not restrict the research to popular stocks or well-known companies.

For every relevant X and LinkedIn post, capture:
- Company name and NSE/BSE ticker.
- Author name and profile link.
- Original post URL.
- Publication date.
- Main investment thesis.
- Specific revenue or growth projections.
- Supporting evidence.
- Whether the post contains factual information, management commentary, an analyst estimate, or personal opinion.

**Important:** Do not simply summarise social media sentiment. Read the actual posts and analyse their underlying claims. Identify recurring investment themes and companies being discussed by multiple independent credible sources.

## 2. MANAGEMENT GUIDANCE AND USD 1 BILLION REVENUE TARGET

Identify companies that meet either of the following categories:

**Category A – Explicit Revenue Target**

Companies whose management has publicly indicated:
- USD 1 Billion annual revenue.
- ₹8,000 crore–₹10,000 crore or higher revenue.
- A comparable large-scale revenue ambition by FY29 or FY30.

**Category B – Strong Revenue Growth Potential**

Companies that may reach USD 1 Billion annual revenue by FY29/FY30 based on:
- Current revenue base.
- Expected CAGR.
- Capacity expansion.
- New business verticals.
- Order-book growth.
- Acquisitions.
- Export opportunities.
- Management commentary.
- Industry growth.

Clearly distinguish between management-confirmed targets and your own estimated projections.

Do not classify a company as having a USD 1 Billion target simply because an investor on X or LinkedIn has made that claim.

For each shortlisted company, create two separate assessments:

**Positive thesis**
- What are investors and analysts excited about?
- What is the growth trigger?
- What is the addressable market?
- Why could revenue accelerate?

**Negative thesis**
- What are the concerns raised on X and LinkedIn?
- Are there execution challenges?

- Are margins declining?

- Are there corporate governance concerns?

## 10. RESEARCH QUALITY AND SOURCE VALIDATION

Follow these strict rules:

1. Prioritise primary sources: NSE/BSE filings, annual reports, investor presentations, earnings calls, and management interviews.
2. Include direct links to X posts and LinkedIn posts.
3. Include publication dates for all important claims.
4. Cross-check social media claims against company disclosures.
5. Separate facts, management guidance, broker estimates, and your own projections.
6. Do not fabricate revenue targets or growth estimates.
7. Do not assume that a large order book automatically converts into revenue.
8. Flag companies with aggressive expansion but weak balance sheets.
9. Avoid promotional posts and unsupported stock recommendations.
10. Include companies with negative sentiment if the underlying business outlook is strong, but explain the risks.

## FINAL OBJECTIVE

I am looking for potential future large-scale businesses in the Indian stock market.

The most important discovery is:

**Which Indian listed companies could become ₹8,000–₹10,000 crore+ annual revenue businesses by FY29/FY30, and which management teams have a credible roadmap to achieve that scale?**

Give preference to companies where the market may not yet have fully recognised the revenue opportunity.

Conclude with three separate lists:

A. **Confirmed Billion-Dollar Aspirants:** Management has explicitly communicated the target.

B. **High-Conviction Billion-Dollar Candidates:** Strong financial, operational, and industry evidence supports the possibility.

C. **Emerging Multibaggers to Track:** Smaller companies with promising new verticals and potentially exponential growth, but with higher execution uncertainty.

Be analytical, sceptical, evidence-driven, and forward-looking. I want institutional-quality research, not a generic list of popular stocks.
